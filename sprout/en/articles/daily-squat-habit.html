How to make squats a daily habit

Do a small, fixed number of squats at the same point in your day, every day. In the best-known study of habit formation, a new daily behaviour took a median of 66 days to become automatic, and missing a single day did not set people back.

How long a habit takes

Researchers at University College London followed 96 people who each chose a new daily behaviour, such as a short exercise after breakfast. Among those whose behaviour did become a habit, it took a median of 66 days to feel automatic, with a range from 18 days to a projected 254. Simple behaviours became habits faster than demanding ones. The often-repeated "21 days" has no research behind it.

Missing a day is not failing

In the same study, missing one opportunity did not noticeably slow habit formation. Being inconsistent over a longer stretch did. The practical rule: skip a day if you must, but not two in a row.

Tie it to something you already do

The participants repeated their behaviour in the same situation each day. Pick a fixed cue: after you brush your teeth, while the coffee brews, before the shower. "After breakfast I do 20 squats" works better than "I will exercise more".

Keep it small enough to do on a bad day

Squats suit this well. They need no equipment, no change of clothes and no travel, and twenty take about a minute. The World Health Organization's guidelines say that every bit of activity counts, with no minimum length for a session, so a short daily set is real exercise.

Let yourself see it

A record of what you have done makes a streak visible and makes the next day easier to start. A mark on a calendar works.

Where Sprout comes in

Sprout is a free iPhone app built around this. The camera counts your squats, the first five water the day's seed, and when you reach your daily goal the plant flowers. Every day you train stays as a plant in your garden, so after a few weeks the garden shows how often you have trained. Friends can visit each other's gardens and leave a butterfly with a few words.
