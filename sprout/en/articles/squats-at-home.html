Are squats at home good for you?

Yes. A bodyweight squat is muscle-strengthening exercise for the largest muscles in the body, it needs no equipment and about one square metre of floor, and it counts toward what the World Health Organization recommends for adults.

What the WHO recommends

The World Health Organization's guidelines say adults should do muscle-strengthening activities that involve the major muscle groups on two or more days a week, in addition to 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity. The same guidelines say that some activity is better than none, and they no longer require activity to come in bouts of at least ten minutes. A few sets of squats in the living room count.

What strength training does for health

A 2022 analysis in the British Journal of Sports Medicine pooled sixteen long-term studies. People who did muscle-strengthening activities had a 10 to 17 percent lower risk of dying from any cause, and of cardiovascular disease, cancer and diabetes, than people who did none. The benefit was largest at roughly 30 to 60 minutes a week. The authors rate the certainty of the evidence as low: these are observational studies, so they show a link, not proof that the exercise caused it.

Why squats in particular

A squat works the thighs, the glutes and the calves together, and the trunk has to hold you upright. It is the movement of sitting down and standing up, which people need for as long as they live. It can be made easier (squat to a chair) or harder (slower, deeper, one leg) without buying anything.

Short bouts count

In a small trial at the University of Toronto, twelve adults sat for seven and a half hours. When they broke up the sitting every 30 minutes with 15 bodyweight squats, their muscles used the protein from their meals more efficiently than when they sat without a break. It was a one-day study, so it says nothing certain about long-term effects, but it supports a simple idea: a few squats, often, are worth doing.

Who should be careful

If you have a knee, hip or back injury, a heart condition, or you are unsure whether exercise is safe for you, ask a doctor or a physiotherapist first. Pain in a joint is a reason to stop.

Where Sprout comes in

Sprout is a free iPhone app that counts your squats with the camera. Every squat makes the day's plant grow, and every day you train becomes a plant in a garden. It does not make squats healthier. It makes them easier to come back to tomorrow.
