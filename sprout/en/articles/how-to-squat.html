How to do a squat correctly at home

Stand with your feet about shoulder width apart, sit back and down until your hips are at about knee height, keep your heels on the floor and your chest up, and stand back up. That is a squat.

Step by step

  1. Feet: shoulder width apart or slightly wider, toes turned out a little.
  2. Down: push your hips back as if sitting on a low chair, and bend your knees. Your knees point the same way as your toes.
  3. Depth: go down until your hips are at about knee height, or as far as you can with your heels on the floor and your back straight.
  4. Up: press through your whole foot and stand up fully. Breathe in on the way down and out on the way up.

The most common mistakes

  • Heels lifting. Widen your stance a little, or do not go as deep until your ankles loosen up.
  • Knees falling inwards. Push them out over your toes.
  • Rounding the back. Look straight ahead and keep your chest up. Holding your arms out in front helps with balance.
  • Stopping high. A squat that only bends the knees a little works the legs much less. If full depth is hard, squat down to a chair and stand up again.

Are deep squats bad for the knees?

For healthy knees, the research says no. A 2013 review in Sports Medicine went through more than 160 studies and concluded that, contrary to a common concern, deep squats do not increase the risk of injury to the knee's passive structures when the technique is sound and the load is built up gradually. That finding is about healthy joints. If you have a knee injury or osteoarthritis, ask a physiotherapist how deep you should go.

Make it easier or harder

Easier: squat to a chair, or hold on to a door frame. Harder: take three seconds on the way down, pause for two at the bottom, or try a split squat with one foot behind you.

Where Sprout comes in

Sprout is a free iPhone app that counts squats with the camera. Lean the phone against a wall and step back so your whole body is in the picture. A squat counts when your hips come down to about knee height and you stand back up; if you stop too high, the app asks you to go lower and does not count it. The picture is analysed on the phone and nothing is recorded.
