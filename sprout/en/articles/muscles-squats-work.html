What muscles do squats work?

Squats mainly work the quadriceps at the front of the thigh and the gluteus maximus in the buttocks. The hamstrings, the calves and the muscles of the trunk help to control and steady the movement.

The muscles that do the lifting

  • Quadriceps. The four muscles at the front of the thigh straighten the knee. They do most of the work of standing back up.
  • Gluteus maximus. The large muscle of the buttocks straightens the hip. Studies summarised in a 2010 review in the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research found it more active in deeper squats.

The muscles that steady you

  • Hamstrings. The muscles at the back of the thigh work together with the quadriceps and help to stabilise the knee.
  • Calves. They control the ankle as the knees travel forward and keep the heels down.
  • Trunk. The muscles along the spine and the abdominal muscles hold the upper body upright.

How depth and stance change it

A squat that stops high works mostly the quadriceps, over a short range. Going down until the hips are at about knee height brings the glutes in more. A wider stance with the toes turned out shifts some of the work to the inner thighs and the glutes. None of these is the correct one; they are variations of the same exercise.

What squats leave out

Squats do little for the arms, the chest and the upper back. The World Health Organization recommends muscle-strengthening activity for all the major muscle groups on two or more days a week, so a push and a pull for the upper body, such as push-ups and rows, belong alongside them.

Where Sprout comes in

Sprout is a free iPhone app that counts bodyweight squats with the camera and grows a plant from them. It counts a squat when your hips come down to about knee height, which is the depth where the glutes join in.
