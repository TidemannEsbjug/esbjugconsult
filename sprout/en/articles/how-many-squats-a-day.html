How many squats a day should you do?

No health authority gives a number of squats per day. A sensible range for a healthy adult is 20 to 50 bodyweight squats a day, split into sets. If you are starting from nothing, start with 10.

What the guidelines actually say

The World Health Organization counts in days and minutes, not repetitions: muscle-strengthening activity for the major muscle groups on two or more days a week. The research on strength training points the same way. In a 2022 analysis of sixteen long-term studies, the lowest risk of early death was found at about 30 to 60 minutes of muscle-strengthening activity a week, and doing more than that did not clearly help further.

Turning minutes into squats

A calm bodyweight squat takes three to four seconds. Fifty squats, with short rests between sets, is about five minutes. Done every day, that is roughly 35 minutes a week of work for the legs, which sits inside the range the research points to. This is our own arithmetic, not a finding from a study, and squats alone do not train the upper body.

A way to build up

  • Week 1: 10 squats a day, in one or two sets. Use a chair behind you if you need it.
  • Weeks 2 to 3: 20 a day, as two sets of 10.
  • Weeks 4 to 6: 30 to 50 a day, in sets of 10 to 15.
  • After that: make them harder, not more numerous. Go slower, go deeper, or pause at the bottom.

Is every day too often?

For bodyweight squats at this volume, daily is fine for most healthy people. Sore muscles in the first week are normal and pass. Pain in a joint is different: stop, and rest a day or two. If you add weight or go close to your limit, give the legs a day between sessions.

Do 100 a day do more?

They build more endurance in the legs. There is no good evidence that they do more for health than 50, and a number you can keep for months is worth more than one you keep for a week.
