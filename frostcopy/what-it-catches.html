Honest overview

What FrostCopy catches, and what it does not

FrostCopy replaces personal information in a copy of your documents, on your own computer. Some things it catches almost every time, others slip through more often. The chart shows what percentage slips through, with what FrostCopy is best at first.

Applies to FrostCopy as the program was on November 18, 2026. Measured with every group turned on, on the text in 183 files in made-up US office folders: Word, PDF, email, Excel, CSV and text files. The folders were written without FrostCopy being adapted to them, and nobody who works on FrostCopy has read them. Only the sums are known to us. Some corrections were made after we saw the sums, so the numbers can be a little better than on entirely new documents.

What percentage slips through in text

The scale goes from 0 to 100%. A shorter bar is better. Hover over a line, or press it, to see an explanation and an example.

020406080100%

The count varies from type to type because ordinary documents are like that: a name appears many times, an account number often only once. That is why we tested 1,109 names, and only 26 account numbers. The percentage makes the types comparable, and numbers built on few examples are less certain. Hover over a line to see the count.

All types together: 166 of 2,847 values remained (5.8%).

Images and scanned pages

We have no numbers for images in the US edition yet. In images FrostCopy first has to read the text, and that gives more mistakes than in ordinary text. Faces are pixelated, and we have not measured how well that works. Always look over images and scanned pages yourself.

✓ Does well

  • Email addresses: none remained in ordinary text in the tests.
  • Phone numbers: 2.4% remained in the tests (6 of 245).
  • Names: under 4% remained, and the same person gets the same code in the whole folder.
  • Also cleans what you do not see: comments, tracked changes and document properties.
  • Everything happens on your own computer, and the originals are not touched.

! Does poorly

  • Other private information, such as family, money and conflicts: around 12% remains.
  • Health and addresses: around 7% remains in text.
  • Images and scanned pages: more mistakes than in text. Signatures and QR codes stay, and handwriting is only replaced if FrostCopy manages to read it.
  • Replaces too much: around 11% of the harmless information is replaced as well.
  • Does not remove what can point to a person indirectly, such as job title, place, age and an event.
  • Is made for English. Health and private sentences in other languages are caught less well.

Almost alwaysWhat FrostCopy catches almost always

This remained least often in ordinary text in the test.

Email and phone

These are replaced almost every time. In the test no email addresses remained, and 6 of 245 phone numbers.

ExampleAn email from a parent with a cell phone number and an email address normally gets both replaced in the copy.

Same person, same code

The information is replaced with codes such as [Person 4829133], so the copy can still be read. The same person gets the same code in the whole folder, and with the same key the code is the same the next time too.

Example“Karen” and “Karen Nelson” both become [Person 4829133] in the same folder. If there are two Karens in the folder, look over which one “Karen” alone was given.

Places you do not see

FrostCopy also cleans what you do not see when you open the file: who wrote a comment, tracked changes, document properties and the preview image. Macros and embedded objects are taken out. This has not been measured separately.

ExampleA Word file where the caseworker wrote a comment gets the name replaced in the comment too, and in the text.

The originals and your own computer

The originals are not touched. FrostCopy makes a new folder with cleaned copies next to them, and the documents are not sent anywhere. The copy always ends up next to the original, so if the folder is in OneDrive or Dropbox, the copy ends up there too.

ExampleYou choose the folder “Cases”. FrostCopy makes “Cases-clean” next to it, and “Cases” is as before.

UsuallyWhat FrostCopy usually catches

This is caught in most cases, and some of it slips through.

Names

Names are usually replaced. A name that is found in one place is replaced everywhere in the folder. Just under 4 in 100 names remained in the test.

Example“Meeting with Owen Baker and his mother” becomes “Meeting with [Person 1180342] and his mother”.

Social Security numbers and other ID numbers

Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, passport numbers and insurance IDs have no check digit that FrostCopy can test. It goes by the written form, such as 987-65-4321, and by labels and column headers. Just under 5% remained in the test. Card numbers, routing numbers, NPIs and VINs are tested against their check digits.

ExampleA nine-digit number under the column header “SSN” is replaced. Nine digits with no label in the middle of a sentence can stay.

Health written in common terms

Diagnoses, sick leave and medication are caught most often. Still, between 6 and 7% of the health information remained in the test.

Example“She has type 2 diabetes” is caught. “He is out a lot because his stomach cannot take the stress” can stay.

Addresses and case numbers

Addresses are on from the start, and about 7% remained. Case numbers are off from the start. When you turn them on, between 5 and 6% remained.

ExampleA home address in a decision letter is replaced. A claim number stays unless Case numbers is turned on.

More missesWhere FrostCopy misses more often

Here more is left, or FrostCopy replaces more than it needs to.

Private things written in everyday words

Family conflicts, who a child lives with, money trouble and complaints described in a person's own words stay more often than names and numbers. Around 12% of this kind of information remained in the test.

Example“Mom and Dad fight every weekend, and the money ran out in March” can stay even though the names are replaced.

Names that are hard to recognize

Initials without periods, short or rare first names, names in lowercase in a chat, and names that are also ordinary words (Baker, Young, Grace, May) can be left, or FrostCopy asks you about them.

Example“Spoke with KB about the case” and “call grace tomorrow” can stay, while “Karen Baker” is replaced.

It replaces things that could have stayed

In the test, 163 of 1,464 harmless values were replaced as well (11.1%). Most were names that should have stayed and ordinary text taken to be private.

ExampleThe caseworker's name under “Sincerely” is replaced. If you want it to stay, set it to “Keep”.

A name is replaced everywhere

A name is replaced in the whole folder. If someone has the same name as a company or a place, the company or the place can be replaced too.

ExampleIf a client is named Baker, “Baker County” can be replaced too.

Other languages than English

FrostCopy is made for American English. Names, numbers and email addresses are caught in other languages too. Spanish names are in the name lists. Health and private sentences in Spanish and other languages are caught much less well.

ExampleA note in Spanish about an employee's illness can keep the health information, while the name and the phone number are replaced.

Not includedWhat FrostCopy does not do

This is not part of the cleaning.

Job title, age and the context

Even with the names gone, someone who knows the case can often work out who it is about from the job title, the place, the age and what happened. FrostCopy does not judge that.

Example“The principal of the only school in town went on sick leave after the fire in May” points to one person with no name in it.

Attachments that are not text

In email, attachments such as PDF, Word files and images are taken out of the copy and must be cleaned as separate files. The copy says so.

ExampleAn email with a scanned form attached becomes an email with a note that the attachment was removed.

Some files change form

A cleaned PDF looks like the original, and each page is an image with the cleaned text underneath. Outlook and Apple Mail messages become .eml, and macros are taken out.

ExampleA PDF with a filled-in form can still be searched, and the fields cannot be filled in again.

It does not decide whether a copy meets a rule

FrostCopy removes or replaces what it finds. It does not decide whether a copy meets any legal standard, such as a de-identification standard, a court's redaction rule or a public records exemption. As long as the original, a code list or a key exists, a copy can be linked back to the people in it.

ExampleA cleaned extract for a researcher must still be assessed by your office, even though the names are replaced.

ImagesImages and scanned pages

In images FrostCopy first has to read the text, and that gives more mistakes than in ordinary text. We have no numbers for the US edition yet.

Faces

Faces are pixelated by default. Small faces, faces from the side and partly hidden faces can be missed. A face FrostCopy does not find stays, with no warning. FrostCopy does not recognize who anyone is.

ExampleTiny profile pictures in a member list can stay without being pixelated. So look over images with people in them.

Only text and faces

FrostCopy only sees text it manages to read, and faces. Signatures, QR codes, barcodes and tattoos stay. Handwriting and license plates are only replaced if FrostCopy manages to read them.

ExampleA scanned contract gets the printed names replaced, and the handwritten signature stays.

Mac, Windows and Linux

Windows and Linux read images and find faces with other tools than Mac, so the result there can be different. On Windows and Linux the text recognition is for English.

ExampleA photo of a filled-in form taken with a phone gives fewer hits than the same form as a Word file.

Your responsibilityWhat you have to do yourself

FrostCopy does much of the work, and this part is yours.

Read the copy through

Read the copy through before you share it, especially cases about individuals. FrostCopy catches most names and numbers, and it cannot promise that everything private is gone.

ExampleBefore you send a child welfare case to an expert, you page through the copy and look for sentences about the family that still say too much.

Think about what you turn off

What you turn off, FrostCopy mostly leaves in. Dates, employer, occupation, age and case numbers are off from the start.

ExampleIf you turn off “Sensitive” to keep the clinical text, every diagnosis stays.

Mark what FrostCopy did not find

If FrostCopy misses a name or a number, click it and hide it. It is then hidden in the whole folder, and FrostCopy remembers it for the next folder.

ExampleYou see that “Mira” was missed in one letter. You click the name, and it is hidden everywhere in the folder.

Clean files that are held back

If the final check finds something that should have been hidden, or a part it cannot read, the copy is not saved. The file is listed in the report, and you have to clean it another way.

ExampleA Word document with an image inside that FrostCopy cannot read gets no cleaned copy.

Delete the backup

By default FrostCopy makes an encrypted backup of the originals on the computer. It contains the originals with all the personal information, and it stays until you delete it under “Backups”. FrostCopy reminds you of backups older than 30 days.

ExampleIf you have cleaned a folder of patient notes, an encrypted copy of the notes stays in FrostCopy's own folder until you remove it.

FrostCopy also replaces too much

In the test, 163 of 1,464 harmless values were replaced (11.1%). That was 63 of 513 names that should have stayed, 71 of 502 other pieces of text, 11 of 150 addresses, 6 of 66 company IDs, 4 of 148 phone numbers and 3 of 53 email addresses. A copy with too much replaced is less useful, so look over what FrostCopy has marked and keep what should stay.

How we measured

We measured FrostCopy on made-up test folders written with AI, and these numbers are from a set that nobody who works on FrostCopy has read: 2,847 pieces of personal information and 1,464 pieces of information that should have stayed, in ordinary text. Each one is checked against an answer key, and we only see the sums. A leak means the information can still be read in the copy. The measuring tool does not read images, so 252 values in images are not counted. One file was held back by the final check and got no copy. The 44 values in it are not counted either.

The set is not entirely untouched: it has been used many times for sums, and corrections have been made after we saw them. We have also tried FrostCopy on real public documents. On a set that others had labeled and that no rule had seen, more remained (about 15 in 100 of the labeled values), and more was replaced than needed. Made-up folders are easier than real ones.

Everything is measured with the engine on Mac. It has not been measured in the same way on Windows or Linux.

For ITThe technical details

For those who want to know how the engine works.

The engine in brief
  • The engine has three parts: US rules with check digits and name lists, a small sentence reader for private sentences, and text recognition for images and scanned pages.
  • Rules: Social Security numbers, taxpayer IDs, EINs, driver's license, passport, Medicare and Medicaid numbers go by written form, labels and column headers. Card numbers (Luhn), routing numbers, NPIs, DEA numbers and VINs are tested against their check digits. IBAN has its own check.
  • The sentence reader is a word-based reader plus a model based on distilbert-base-cased, fine-tuned for FrostCopy, that runs on the processor in Rust.
  • No large language model and no chatbot in the program.
  • Images: Apple Vision on Mac. Windows' own text recognition and Tesseract with English on Windows, and Tesseract with English on Linux. Faces: Apple Vision on Mac, small face models (YuNet and UltraFace) elsewhere.
Network, copies and storage
  • The program has no HTTP library and no network client. Buying a license only opens our website in your own browser.
  • The copy folder is always placed next to the original with “-clean” after the name, and a new one is made (“-clean 2”) if it exists, so nothing is overwritten.
  • A final check reads the whole copy before it is saved. If anything that was found is still in it, or a part cannot be read, the copy is not saved, and the report names the file. This check can also miss things.
  • The commands that show what the program talks to are on the page for IT.
No automatic cleaning catches everything.

FrostCopy catches most names and numbers, and less of what is private in other ways. So read the copy through before you share it. You are responsible for what you share, and FrostCopy does not decide whether a copy meets any legal standard.

If you want to check that FrostCopy sends nothing out, the commands are on the page for the IT department.

If you find something FrostCopy should have caught, we would like to hear about it. We update this page when the engine changes.
