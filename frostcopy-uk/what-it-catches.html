Honest overview

What FrostCopy catches, and what it does not

FrostCopy replaces personal information in a copy of your documents, on your own computer. Some things it catches almost every time, others slip through more often. The chart shows what percentage slips through, with what FrostCopy is best at first.

Applies to FrostCopy UK as the program was on 21 November 2026. Measured with every group turned on, on the text in 145 files in five made-up British office folders: youth justice in Manchester, children’s services in Cardiff, immigration solicitors in Birmingham, adult support and protection in Edinburgh and debt advice in Belfast. Word, PDF, email, Excel, CSV and text files. The folders were written without FrostCopy being adapted to them, and nobody who works on FrostCopy has read them. Only the sums are known to us.

What percentage slips through in text

The scale goes from 0 to 100%. A shorter bar is better. Hover over a line, or press it, to see an explanation and an example. For this version we have the sums for ID numbers, names and case numbers, and the total for everything.

The count varies from type to type because ordinary documents are like that: a name appears many times, an ID number often only once. The percentage makes the types comparable, and numbers built on few examples are less certain. Hover over a line to see the count.

All types together: 5.3% of the 3,253 values that should have been removed remained. That includes addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, health information and other private sentences, which we have not broken out for this version.

Images and scanned pages

We have no numbers for images in the British edition yet. In images FrostCopy first has to read the text, and that gives more mistakes than in ordinary text. Faces are pixelated, and we have not measured how well that works. Always look over images and scanned pages yourself.

✓ Does well

  • British ID numbers, such as National Insurance and NHS numbers: 2 of 124 remained in the test.
  • Names: about 2 in 100 remained, and the same person gets the same code in the whole folder.
  • Knows British forms: postcodes, UK phone numbers, sort codes with account numbers, number plates and DVLA licence numbers.
  • Also cleans what you do not see: comments, tracked changes and document properties.
  • Everything happens on your own computer, and the originals are not touched.

! Does poorly

  • Case and reference numbers: about 15 in 100 remained when Case numbers was turned on.
  • Private things written in everyday words, such as family, money and conflicts, stay more often than names and numbers.
  • Replaces too much: 21% of the harmless information was replaced as well, often office addresses and the names of other services.
  • Images and scanned pages: more mistakes than in text. Signatures and QR codes stay, and handwriting is only replaced if FrostCopy manages to read it.
  • Does not remove what can point to a person indirectly, such as job title, place, age and an event.
  • Is made for English. Health and private sentences in Welsh and other languages are caught less well.

Almost alwaysWhat FrostCopy catches almost always

This remained least often in ordinary text in the test.

British ID numbers

National Insurance numbers are recognised by their form, with or without spaces. NHS, CHI and UTR numbers are tested against their check digits, so a random ten-digit number is not taken for one of them. Labels and column headers such as “NI No”, “NHS Number”, “CHI”, “BRP” and “Share code” are recognised too. In the test, 2 of 124 ID numbers remained.

ExampleA column headed “NHS No” in a spreadsheet of referrals is replaced from top to bottom.

Same person, same code

The information is replaced with codes such as [Person 4829133], so the copy can still be read. The same person gets the same code in the whole folder, and with the same key the code is the same the next time too.

Example“Karen” and “Karen Nelson” both become [Person 4829133] in the same folder. If there are two Karens in the folder, look over which one “Karen” alone was given.

Places you do not see

FrostCopy also cleans what you do not see when you open the file: who wrote a comment, tracked changes, document properties and the preview image. Macros and embedded objects are taken out. This has not been measured separately.

ExampleA Word file where the social worker wrote a comment gets the name replaced in the comment too, and in the text.

The originals and your own computer

The originals are not touched. FrostCopy makes a new folder with cleaned copies next to them, and the documents are not sent anywhere. The copy always ends up next to the original, so if the folder is in OneDrive or Dropbox, the copy ends up there too.

ExampleYou choose the folder “Cases”. FrostCopy makes “Cases-clean” next to it, and “Cases” is as before.

UsuallyWhat FrostCopy usually catches

This is caught in most cases, and some of it slips through.

Names

Names are usually replaced. A name that is found in one place is replaced everywhere in the folder. About 2 in 100 names remained in the test. Titles such as Cllr, PC, DS and Sgt are recognised in front of a name.

Example“Meeting with Owen Baker and his mother” becomes “Meeting with [Person 1180342] and his mother”.

Contact details and addresses

UK mobile and landline numbers, email addresses, postcodes and street addresses are on from the start. We have not broken out their numbers for this version; they are part of the total of 5.3%.

ExampleA home address with a postcode in a letter is replaced. “The flat above the chip shop” can stay.

Health and sensitive information

Diagnoses, sick leave, medication and the British words for them (GP, A&E, fit note, sectioned, CAMHS) are caught most often. So are words for criminal records, social care, immigration status and debt, such as “released under investigation”, “child protection plan”, “no recourse to public funds” and “debt relief order”.

Example“She has been signed off with stress since March” is caught. “He is out a lot because his stomach cannot take it” can stay.

More missesWhere FrostCopy misses more often

Here more is left, or FrostCopy replaces more than it needs to.

Case and reference numbers

Case numbers are off from the start. When you turn them on, about 15 in 100 remained in the test. Each office writes its references its own way, so a reference with no label can stay.

Example“Case ref: 2026/0418” is replaced. The same number alone in a file name can stay.

Private things written in everyday words

Family conflicts, who a child lives with, money trouble and complaints described in a person’s own words stay more often than names and numbers.

Example“Mum and Dad row every weekend, and the money ran out in March” can stay even though the names are replaced.

Names that are hard to recognise

Initials without full stops, short or rare first names, names in lower case in a chat, and names that are also ordinary words (Baker, Young, Grace, May) can be left, or FrostCopy asks you about them.

Example“Spoke with KB about the case” and “ring grace tomorrow” can stay, while “Karen Baker” is replaced.

It replaces things that could have stayed

In the test, 21% of the harmless values were replaced as well. Most were office addresses, the names of other services and ordinary text taken to be private. Mark one as “Keep” once, and FrostCopy keeps it from then on.

ExampleThe address of the council’s own office in a letter footer is replaced. Set it to “Always keep”, and it stays in every folder after that.

A name is replaced everywhere

A name is replaced in the whole folder. If someone has the same name as a company or a place, the company or the place can be replaced too.

ExampleIf a client is named Bolton, “Bolton Council” can be replaced too.

Other languages than English

FrostCopy is made for English as it is written in the UK. Names, numbers and email addresses are caught in other languages too. Health and private sentences in Welsh, Polish and other languages are caught much less well.

ExampleA note in Welsh about a tenant’s illness can keep the health information, while the name and the phone number are replaced.

Not includedWhat FrostCopy does not do

This is not part of the cleaning.

Job title, age and the context

Even with the names gone, someone who knows the case can often work out who it is about from the job title, the place, the age and what happened. FrostCopy does not judge that.

Example“The head teacher of the only primary school in the village went off sick after the fire in May” points to one person with no name in it.

Attachments that are not text

In email, attachments such as PDF, Word files and images are taken out of the copy and must be cleaned as separate files. The copy says so.

ExampleAn email with a scanned form attached becomes an email with a note that the attachment was removed.

Some files change form

A cleaned PDF looks like the original, and each page is an image with the cleaned text underneath. Outlook and Apple Mail messages become .eml, and macros are taken out.

ExampleA PDF with a filled-in form can still be searched, and the fields cannot be filled in again.

It does not decide whether a copy meets a rule

FrostCopy removes or replaces what it finds. It does not decide whether a copy meets any legal standard, such as anonymisation under the UK GDPR, a court’s rules on redaction or an exemption under the Freedom of Information Act. As long as the original, a code list or a key exists, a copy can be linked back to the people in it.

ExampleA cleaned extract for a researcher must still be assessed by your information governance team, even though the names are replaced.

ImagesImages and scanned pages

In images FrostCopy first has to read the text, and that gives more mistakes than in ordinary text. We have no numbers for the British edition yet.

Faces

Faces are pixelated by default. Small faces, faces from the side and partly hidden faces can be missed. A face FrostCopy does not find stays, with no warning. FrostCopy does not recognise who anyone is.

ExampleTiny profile pictures in a staff list can stay without being pixelated. So look over images with people in them.

Only text and faces

FrostCopy only sees text it manages to read, and faces. Signatures, QR codes, barcodes and tattoos stay. Handwriting and number plates are only replaced if FrostCopy manages to read them.

ExampleA scanned tenancy agreement gets the printed names replaced, and the handwritten signature stays.

Mac, Windows and Linux

Windows and Linux read images and find faces with other tools than Mac, so the result there can be different. On Windows and Linux the text recognition is for English.

ExampleA photo of a filled-in form taken with a phone gives fewer hits than the same form as a Word file.

Your responsibilityWhat you have to do yourself

FrostCopy does much of the work, and this part is yours.

Read the copy through

Read the copy through before you share it, especially cases about individuals. FrostCopy catches most names and numbers, and it cannot promise that everything private is gone.

ExampleBefore you send a subject access reply, you page through the copy and look for sentences about other family members that still say too much.

Think about what you turn off

What you turn off, FrostCopy mostly leaves in. Dates, employer, occupation, age and case numbers are off from the start.

ExampleIf you turn off “Sensitive” to keep the clinical text, every diagnosis stays.

Mark what FrostCopy did not find

If FrostCopy misses a name or a number, click it and hide it. It is then hidden in the whole folder, and FrostCopy remembers it for the next folder.

ExampleYou see that “Mira” was missed in one letter. You click the name, and it is hidden everywhere in the folder.

Clean files that are held back

If the final check finds something that should have been hidden, or a part it cannot read, the copy is not saved. The file is listed in the report, and you have to clean it another way.

ExampleA Word document with an image inside that FrostCopy cannot read gets no cleaned copy.

Delete the backup

By default FrostCopy makes an encrypted backup of the originals on the computer. It contains the originals with all the personal information, and it stays until you delete it under “Backups”. FrostCopy reminds you of backups older than 30 days.

ExampleIf you have cleaned a folder of patient notes, an encrypted copy of the notes stays in FrostCopy’s own folder until you remove it.

FrostCopy also replaces too much

In the test, 21.0% of the 1,982 harmless values were replaced as well. Most were office addresses, the names of other services and organisations, and ordinary text taken to be private. A copy with too much replaced is less useful, so look over what FrostCopy has marked and keep what should stay. What you keep once, FrostCopy keeps in later folders too.

How we measured

We measured FrostCopy on made-up British test folders written with AI, and these numbers are from a set that nobody who works on FrostCopy has read: five offices, 145 files, 3,253 pieces of personal information and 1,982 pieces of information that should have stayed, in ordinary text. Each one is checked against an answer key, and we only see the sums. A leak means the information can still be read in the copy. The measuring tool does not read images, so values in images are not counted.

Made-up folders are easier than real ones, so expect more to remain in your own documents. Five offices are still few, and we add more test folders as the engine changes.

Everything is measured with the engine on Mac. It has not been measured in the same way on Windows or Linux.

For ITThe technical details

For those who want to know how the engine works.

The engine in brief
  • The engine has three parts: British rules with check digits and name lists, a small sentence reader for private sentences, and text recognition for images and scanned pages.
  • Rules: National Insurance numbers go by their written form, labels and column headers. NHS, CHI and Health and Care numbers, UTRs, VAT numbers and pupil numbers (UPN) are tested against their check digits. Company and charity numbers, PAYE references, DVLA and DVA licence numbers, share codes, BRPs, sort codes with account numbers, number plates and postcodes have their own rules. Card numbers (Luhn) and IBAN have their own checks.
  • The sentence reader is a word-based reader plus a model based on distilbert-base-cased, fine-tuned for FrostCopy, that runs on the processor in Rust.
  • No large language model and no chatbot in the program.
  • Images: Apple Vision on Mac. Windows’ own text recognition and Tesseract with English on Windows, and Tesseract with English on Linux. Faces: Apple Vision on Mac, small face models (YuNet and UltraFace) elsewhere.
Network, copies and storage
  • The program has no HTTP library and no network client. Buying a licence only opens our website in your own browser.
  • The copy folder is always placed next to the original with “-clean” after the name, and a new one is made (“-clean 2”) if it exists, so nothing is overwritten.
  • A final check reads the whole copy before it is saved. If anything that was found is still in it, or a part cannot be read, the copy is not saved, and the report names the file. This check can also miss things.
  • The commands that show what the program talks to are on the page for IT.
No automatic cleaning catches everything.

FrostCopy catches most names and numbers, and less of what is private in other ways. So read the copy through before you share it. You are responsible for what you share, and FrostCopy does not decide whether a copy meets any legal standard.

If you want to check that FrostCopy sends nothing out, the commands are on the page for the IT department.

If you find something FrostCopy should have caught, we would like to hear about it. We update this page when the engine changes.
